However, my idea has shifted from a large bulky tower with multiple water pumps to a more discrete and compact hydroponic system. </p> <p class = "margin"></p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Base.png"> <p class = "margin"></p> <p> The new system will have detachable baskets that the plants will go into. These will initially contain seeds and rockwool and sit in a pool of water, taking advantage a deep water hydropnics (DWC) system. </p> <p class = "margin"></p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Plantpod.png"> <p> Once complete, this mechanism will have a timed air stone that pumps oxygen into the water, a strength adjusting grow light that measures the amount of sunlight in an area and compensates appropriately by adjusting the brightness of a grow light, and senor that tells the user when their water levels are getting too low. </p> <p class = "margin"></p> <p> Materials <p class = "margin"></p> <p> In order to complete this project, I will need the following: A programmable grow light, 1.5" rockwool squares, plant seeds (herbs or leafy greens), appropriate wiring and microcontroller, phototransistors, liquid level sensors, LEDs, and potentially an airstone pump. The rest of the pieces should be able to be 3d printed or cut in the shop. </p> </p> <p class = "margin"></p> <p> Timeline </p> <p class = "margin"></p> <p> As it stands, I have a rough model for what my product will look like. For the MVP week, I plan to 3d print one of the seed baskets and try to create the adaptive grow light. From there, nailing down the body of the product will be the most important next step. I think I will use a combination of CNC milling and 3d printing to create this. Once it is made, I can move on to making the water level sensors. Finally, if time allows, I can set up an air pump system to oxygenate the water on a timer that turns on periodically throughout the day. </p> </div>
