We decided that because both of our MVPs from prior weeks involved us programming LED strips, that it would be an interesting project to connect these LED strips to a LAN Server. Ideating on what project we could make out of this, we ultimately landed on creating an emotion box adjacent to a mood ring. With this idea in mind, we first had to figure out what shape this box should be, and so in the vein of emotions, we obviously landed on a heart. </p> <p class="margin"> </p> <a download href="HeartBox.dxf" download>Download our laser cutter DXF file!</a> <p class="margin"> </p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Laser Cutout.JPG"> <p class="margin"> </p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Assembled Box.JPG"> <p class="margin"> </p> <p> Now that our box was assembled, we had to figure out what interface we would set up to cause the box to actually change colors and "show us how we're feeling". To do this, we settled on two primary inputs. The first would be button based on our LAN Server. If you are feeling sad, press the button that tells us you are feeling sad and the box will turn blue (if angry, the box turns red, etc.). </p> <p class="margin"> </p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./LanServer.JPG"> <p class="margin"> </p> <a download href="MOODRING.zip" download>Download our code for the entire project!</a> <p class="margin"> </p> <p>And here is how our circuit looked!</p> <p class="margin"> </p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Circuit.JPG"> <p class="margin"> </p> <p class="margin"> </p> <p> The second input that we settled on exactly mimicked the system used by mood rings. Accompanying this, we added a button labeled "Click here to see how you're really feeling." See this in acion below. </p> <p class="margin"> </p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Mood-Ring-Colors-Chart.png"> <p class="margin"> </p> <img width="640" height="640" src="./Finished Box Image.JPG"> <p class="margin"> </p> <video width="640" height="640" controls> <source src="Working Box.mp4" type="video/mp4"> </video> <p class="margin"> </p> <p> Ultimately, this week was a great reminder that sometimes the things we create can be an excellent opportunity to take a step back and not take our engineering projects so seriously!
